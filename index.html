Quy Nhon Family Guide

Sea, culture and food on Vietnam's south-central coast. Written for families visiting in June and July and staying on the Cat Tien coast north of the city, home to resorts such as Maia Resort and The Ocean Resort by Fusion.

Last checked: 17 November 2026. Anything tagged Check before you go may change before your trip.

At a glance

In short

Hot, sunny and calm: the best sea of the year, and the busiest season. Book early, and plan around the midday heat.

Where you are. Quy Nhon is a beach city on Vietnam's south-central coast. Since July 2025 it has been part of Gia Lai province. Older maps, signs and websites still say Binh Dinh; both mean the same place. Local time is UTC+7.

A typical day, hour by hour

  • The sea is at its calmest. June and July are the peak months for boat trips, snorkelling and the islands.
  • Rain is mostly short. Monthly totals are low. Expect the odd shower, not wash-outs.
  • Storms are unlikely. The typhoon season is mainly September to November. If an early storm comes, boat trips are cancelled.
  • It's hot. Do the sea and sightseeing early (sunrise is around 5:15) and late in the afternoon. Spend midday at the resort.
  • It's busy. June and July are the Vietnamese school-holiday peak. Book boats, restaurants and cars ahead, especially for a big group.

Getting there

  • By air: Phu Cat airport (UIH), about 30–45 minutes from the Cat Tien coast. There are daily flights from Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City (Vietnam Airlines, VietJet), and Bamboo Airways several times a week. No scheduled international flights, so connect through Hanoi or Ho Chi Minh City.
  • By train: long-distance trains stop at Dieu Tri, 10 km west of the city and about 35–45 minutes from Cat Tien. Da Nang is 5–5.5 hours away, and Nha Trang 4–4.5 hours.
  • With a big group and luggage, pre-book a van or several 7-seat cars from the airport. Your resort can usually arrange this.

Where you're based and how far things are

In short

The Cat Tien resorts sit close to the best coves, and 25–35 minutes from the city. Sea days are easy. City and inland days need a car and a little planning.

Cat Tien is a quiet stretch of coast about 17 km north of the city, across the Thi Nai lagoon and beyond the Phuong Mai peninsula. It is home to resorts such as Maia Resort and, next door, The Ocean Resort by Fusion. The drive times below start from there.

What this means for planning

  • Easy Under 30 minutes: half-days. Go early and be back for lunch and the pool.
  • Half-day+ About an hour: combine places, such as Banh It plus the city, or a city afternoon plus a seafood dinner.
  • Full day Over an hour: whole-day outings. Ganh Da Dia means 4–4.5 hours of driving for the round trip.
  • For any day trip, book a car with a driver for the whole day.

Getting around

In short

Hire cars with drivers for every outing. Book whole days for anything over an hour away, and book for the whole group in advance.

  • For a large group, book one 16-seat van or two or three 7-seat cars. Separate cars let the group split up for different activities.
  • Cost: a car with driver runs about 1.5–2.2 million VND (US$60–85) a day plus fuel. Vans cost more. Check before you go
  • Booking: through your resort (easiest, with English, usually dearer), through a local transport company, or as part of a private tour that includes the car.
  • Short hops: book in an app. It beats flagging a cab or phoning: the pick-up pin and destination remove the language barrier, and Grab shows the fare before you ride. Pick-ups on the Cat Tien coast may take longer.
    • Grab, including 6-seat cars.
    • Xanh SM electric taxis (app, or hotline 1555).
    • Lado Taxi, a local firm with 4-seat and 7-seat cars (Toyota Innova). Book in the Lado Taxi app (in English) or call 0256 3 666 777. Check before you go

Before each day out

  • Agree the plan, start and finish times, waiting time, and whether fuel, tolls and parking are included.
  • Ask for a driver on Zalo or WhatsApp, so you can message with a translation app.
  • A short written itinerary in Vietnamese helps.

The sea

In short

June and July are the best months for the sea, and the best of it is close to Cat Tien. A private boat for the group is the easy way to see Ky Co and Hon Kho.

Beaches and coves

Ky Co

Easy 15–20 min by road, or 7–15 min by canoe from Nhon Ly

A sheltered turquoise cove with snorkelling nearby at Bai Dua. Very busy on summer mornings. Entry about 100,000–120,000 VND per adult. Check before you go The 2025 typhoon damaged the on-site facilities.

Eo Gio

Easy about 15 min

Clifftop walkways over a wind-swept cove. Best at sunrise. Not for swimming: strong currents. Entry 30,000 VND; a rise is planned.

Hon Kho

Easy 25 min to Nhon Hai pier, then 5–10 min by boat

A small islet with the calmest, shallowest snorkelling reef nearby. At low tide a sandbar path appears through the sea.

Or go by yacht: Son Tung Yacht's Hon Kho tour leaves Tocepo port in the city at 8:00 and is back by 12:00 (about 30 min from Cat Tien). It covers a bay cruise, an hour of snorkelling, SUP and pedal boats at Hon Kho, and a seafood lunch on board. Listed at 1.5–2.5 million VND per adult, depending on deck, children 70%. Up to 49 guests, and a whole-yacht charter is possible for a group. Check before you go The price varies on the page, and we couldn't confirm the vessel's licence.

City beach (Xuan Dieu)

Half-day+ 25–35 min

5 km of sand and a promenade. Lifeguards from 04:30 to 10:00 and from 15:00; none from 10:00 to 15:00. Free life jackets. Free entry.

Boat trips for a big group

  • Private speedboat (recommended). One boat fits a large family group and avoids the crowded shared trips. Listed charter prices: Nhon Ly to Ky Co snorkel, 3 h, about 2.8 million VND; Nhon Hai to Hon Kho, 3 h, 1.5 million; two islands, 4 h, 3.5 million. Check before you go
  • Shared day tours to Ky Co, Eo Gio and Hon Kho, often with a seafood lunch: 635,000–975,000 VND per person direct from Vietnamese operators, or US$37–58 on Klook or GetYourGuide (English listings, free cancellation). Ask whether an English-speaking guide comes along.
  • Ky Co on your own from Nhon Ly pier: 290,000 VND for the return canoe, entry and snorkelling, or 480,000 with a seafood lunch. Canoes run 07:00–11:00 and 13:00–17:00.
  • Use licensed boats only. Since 2025 only licensed speedboats may carry visitors on these routes, and fishing boats may not carry tourists.

On and in the water

  • Snorkelling. Hon Kho is the easiest reef; Ky Co and Bai Dua suit stronger swimmers. Reef condition in 2027 is uncertain after the 2025 typhoon and with a strong El Niño forecast. Check before you go
  • Stand-up paddleboard. Boards for hire at Ky Co (about 100,000 VND an hour) and at Nhon Hai and Nhon Ly (150,000–350,000 VND a session). Early morning is calmest.
  • Night squid fishing Good for teenagers Trips leave Nhon Ly around sunset for lit rafts about 1 km offshore. You fish with rods, and the catch is grilled on board. 280,000–690,000 VND per person shared, or 1.5–3 million VND for a private boat. Cancelled in wind; no refund for an empty night.
  • Dawn with the fishermen. At first light, Nhon Ly fishers haul shore nets from the beach, and visitors can help pull. It's free and informal.
  • Jet skis and parasailing are offered at Ky Co and Hon Kho, but licensing is unclear and there was a fatal accident in 2025. We don't recommend them.

Staying safe

  • Wear a life jacket on every boat. On crowded snorkel rafts, insist on one and avoid the midday slots.
  • Don't swim at Eo Gio. On the city beach, swim between the flags during lifeguard hours.
  • Jellyfish: rinse stings with seawater, not fresh water, then vinegar if you have it. Get help for any breathing difficulty.
  • Wear reef shoes at Ky Co and Hon Kho, where there are sea urchins and rocky entries.
  • UV is extreme: wear rash guards and reef-safe sunscreen, and reapply after every swim.
  • After heavy rain, stay away from city-beach drains for a day or two.

Early-morning hike

Half-day Good for teenagers 25–45 min

Optional, for the energetic. Start at first light and be down by about 8:00, before the heat. Take plenty of water and proper shoes.

  • Vung Chua (about 600 m): the big view over the city and bay. Allow 2–3 hours; steep. About 35–45 min from Cat Tien.
  • Ba Hoa (about 300 m): a city-edge hill locals climb at dawn. The road up is blocked, so it is about 4 km on foot. Access rules are unclear. About 25–35 min.
  • Hikes With Paul: volunteer-led charity hikes on Ba Hoa, Xuan Van or Vung Chua, about 70,000 VND per person. Book at Quy Nhon Sports Bar, 92 An Duong Vuong.

Heavy rain in November 2026 caused landslides near Vung Chua and evacuations around Ba Hoa. Check before you go for trail conditions and whether Paul's hikes still run.

Craft villages

Half-day+

Horse-hair conical hats, rice paper, rice liquor and pottery in the An Nhon area. Hands-on: make rice paper or sew a hat. Local group day tours cost about 690,000 VND per person. Ask about an English guide.

Martial arts

Half-day Good for teenagers

Binh Dinh's own martial art is famous across Vietnam. A half-day tour at a pagoda school includes a demonstration, a short lesson and a lagoon lunch, for 745,000–850,000 VND per person. Ask about group size and an English guide.

Tay Son: Quang Trung Museum and Duong Long

The story of the Tay Son brothers, a war-drum and martial-arts show (about 9:00 and 14:30), and the tallest Cham towers. Museum 50,000 VND, children 25,000. Call ahead to confirm the show.

Good to know

  • Etiquette: cover shoulders and knees at pagodas, take shoes off where others do, and ask before photographing people.
  • Drones: most famous coastal viewpoints are no-fly zones, and drones must be registered and pilots licensed. Leave it at home.
  • ID on boat days: the coast is a legal border area, and piers sometimes check ID. Carry passports or photos of them.

Eating as a big group

  • Seafood village restaurants at Nhon Ly, Nhon Hai and Hai Minh: choose your seafood from the tanks. Agree the price per kilo and watch it weighed before it's cooked. CocoCamp at Nhon Hai is a large seafood restaurant used to groups, near Cat Tien.
  • Book big tables ahead in June and July, through your resort or your driver.
  • Night market (Lê Duẩn street): 17:00–23:00, busiest 19:00–21:00.
  • Ngô Văn Sở food street: snacks and pancakes, busiest 18:00–22:00. Good for grazing in small groups.

Food experiences

  • Cooking class at Nhon Hai fishing village (held at CocoCamp): 08:00–12:00, with a village tour by electric cart, a market visit, and a class making squid pancakes. About 400,000 VND per person. Confirm English and group size.
  • Street-food tours by electric cart: about 1.05–1.15 million VND per person, with daily afternoon-to-evening departures. English versions cost US$50–90 on international booking sites. A big group needs two carts.
  • Craft-village food tours (rice paper, rice liquor, noodles): see Culture. The rice liquor is strong and for adults only.

Choose busy stalls with high turnover, drink bottled or filtered water, and carry a translated allergy card if anyone needs one.

Suggested itinerary

In short

Three relaxed days for late June or early July: cliffs and a city dinner, an island morning and a beach seafood night, then a choose-your-own day. Sea before 11:00, rest at midday, out again in the evening.

Arrival

Land at Phu Cat or arrive at Dieu Tri, then transfer to Cat Tien (30–45 min) by pre-booked van or cars. Swim, then dinner at the resort.

Day 1: cliffs, then a welcome dinner in town

Early (choose one): Eo Gio cliff walk, 15 minutes away. Go at sunrise, around 6:00–7:30: coaches start arriving from about 7:00, and the wind picks up later. Stop for a fresh coconut over the bay. Or a slow morning at the resort pool and beach.

Midday: back at the resort, out of the heat.

About 16:30: drive into the city over the Thi Nai bridge (30–40 min). Welcome dinner at Thao Dien Restaurant. It's in the south of the city, so Ghenh Rang headland, just beyond, makes an easy pre-dinner stop.

Day 2: Hon Kho island, then seafood on the beach at Bai Xep

Morning: drive about 25 minutes to Nhon Hai fishing village and aim to arrive by 8:15–8:30. A private boat crosses to Hon Kho in roughly 5–12 minutes. Walk the island boardwalk and snorkel the shallow reef before the heat and the day-tour crowds arrive.

Lunch: seafood at Nhon Hai village (CocoCamp is used to groups), or on the island if your operator arranges it. Then back to the resort for the afternoon.

Evening: seafood on the beach at Bai Xep, a small fishing village and beach about 13 km south of the city centre. Allow about 45–60 minutes each way from Cat Tien. Book ahead and confirm the venue can seat the whole group.

Alternative evening: a boat out to the floating seafood rafts. Check before you go We couldn't confirm a licensed operator for these.

Booking the Hon Kho boat

  • Licensed boats only. Since 2025, only licensed speedboats may carry visitors between Nhon Hai and Hon Kho, and fishing boats may not carry tourists. A "traditional wooden boat" or longtail may not be licensed, so ask to see the licence.
  • What it costs: a private 3-hour boat runs about 1.5 million VND (around US$60) for the boat, or 300,000–500,000 VND per person for a pier package with snorkel gear and lunch. Check before you go
  • On the island: there's little shade and few facilities. Bring water, hats and reef shoes, and wear life jackets on the boat.

Day 3: choose your own

Morning (pick one, or split the group):

  • Dunes and coast: Phuong Mai sand dunes on the peninsula, roughly 15–25 minutes away, for sandboarding and coastal viewpoints. Go early, because the sand gets very hot by mid-morning. 4x4 dune rides are offered by some local operators. Check before you go
  • Cooking at the villa: a bánh xèo (crispy pancake) and rice-paper rolling class. Ask your resort whether it can arrange one, or join the Nhon Hai village class instead.
  • Pure rest: pool, beach, a book.

Afternoon: back together at the resort to swim and rest.

Late afternoon: drinks at Surf Bar, a café-bar on the sand of the city beach (open 06:00–23:00), then a stroll along the Xuan Dieu waterfront. The city beach faces east, so you'll see dusk light over the bay rather than a sunset over the water.

Departure

Allow 45 minutes to Phu Cat airport. If you're continuing by road, a van can collect you from the resort in the morning.

If you have an extra day

  • Culture: the martial-arts half-day, or the craft villages and Banh It towers.
  • Teenagers: night squid fishing from Nhon Ly (17:30–21:30), or an early-morning hike up Vung Chua or Ba Hoa (see Culture and surroundings).
  • Big day out: Tay Son (museum, drum show and Duong Long, 75–100 min each way) or Ganh Da Dia (about 2–2¼ h each way).

Book before you travel

  • Cars or a van with drivers for each outing
  • The Hon Kho boat (licensed), or the Son Tung yacht tour
  • Dinner tables for the whole group, at Thao Dien and at Bai Xep
  • Any cooking class or dune ride

On the ground

In short

Carry cash for villages and boats, message drivers on Zalo, plan around the heat, and know where the hospital is.

  • Cash. Cards work at resorts, larger restaurants and tour operators. Markets, street food, village restaurants and small boats take cash only (Vietnamese dong). Carry small notes. ATMs are easier to find in the city than on the Cat Tien coast. At ATMs, choose to be charged in VND.
  • Messaging. Most drivers and operators use Zalo; some use WhatsApp. Google Translate with offline Vietnamese helps for written messages.
  • Heat. UV is extreme. Plan around midday, and drink plenty of water.
  • Hospital. Central General Hospital of Gia Lai Province, 106 Nguyễn Huệ, Quy Nhon. It has a paediatric ward. Give drivers the street address: a different "Gia Lai General Hospital" is 160 km away in Pleiku.
  • Clinic. Hòa Đức polyclinic, 04–08 Đinh Công Tráng, 06:30–18:30. Doctor home or hotel visits are also available locally.
  • Place names. Signs and apps may say Gia Lai or Binh Dinh. Both are right.
